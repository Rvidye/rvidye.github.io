Every year, the [High Performance Graphics](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/) (HPG) conference hosts a student competition. About a week before the 2026 competition, [Cem](https://www.cemyuksel.com/) posted in our lab's Slack asking whether anyone was planning to take part.

I wasn't. But I looked at the submission page and thought it might be fun to try something. A week later I had **MayaSabha: Hall of Illusion**, a procedural palace inspired by the [Mayasabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayasabha) from the [*Mahabharata*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata): a mythical hall famous for its grandeur, its illusions, and its deceptive architecture.

The scene is built entirely inside a **[ShaderToy](https://www.shadertoy.com/view/s3BGD1) shader**, using [signed distance fields](https://iquilezles.org/articles/distfunctions/) (SDFs) to build an endlessly repeating palace of arches, columns, reflective floors, glowing portals and crystals. The architecture varies from chamber to chamber, so the hall feels like it goes on forever.

For rendering, I implemented a **progressive Monte Carlo path tracer**: multiple indirect bounces, next-event estimation for the emissive geometry, MIS-style weighting, and glossy and specular BRDF sampling. Everything runs inside a fragment shader, which was an interesting constraint to work with.

Performance was another story.

<Figure src={running} alt="ShaderToy's player showing the portal corridor, with the control bar reading 9.6 fps at 960 by 540.">
  Running in ShaderToy: 9.6 frames per second at 960 × 540.
</Figure>

The shader is reasonably fast for what it's doing, but I wouldn't call it real-time. I experimented with [SVGF](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2017-07_spatiotemporal-variance-guided-filtering-real-time-reconstruction-path-traced) and [ReSTIR](https://benedikt-bitterli.me/restir/) to improve performance and convergence, but neither worked particularly well in my implementation. With the time I had, I decided to prioritize image quality over spending the rest of the competition debugging sampling and denoising.

Somehow, this last-minute experiment won **[second place at the HPG 2026 Student Competition](https://www.highperformancegraphics.org/2026/student-competition/)**.
